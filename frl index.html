Independent research lab / Agent authorization, memory, evaluation

Systems can already rewrite themselves. Authorization has not caught up.

We study how authority, memory, and identity should behave inside software that acts without a human watching each step. What we conclude gets written up, argued with, and published.

Position

The unit of control is not the action.

Most of the field authorizes actions. That works while a model answers questions and stops working the moment the model can change its own policy, spawn a second agent, or inherit a session that was granted to someone else. By the time a harmful action appears, the decision that allowed it happened several steps earlier, in a place nobody was watching.

We think the unit is the operator and the handoff: the mechanism by which a system changes itself, and the moment one agent takes on another's authority. Hold those two above the agent, admit every self-change through a gate, re-bind identity per generation, keep a signed lineage, and a large amount of the remaining safety problem becomes ordinary engineering.

Frontier Research Lab was founded by Binod Kumar. We work in the open, publish negative results, and treat any claim we cannot reproduce as a claim we have not made.

Research

Four questions we are working on now.

Each line below is an active thread with a preprint, a reference implementation, or an evaluation attached to it.

Self-modification

Authorization for self-improving systems

The improvement operator, not the improved behaviour, is what should be authorized. Admission control on every self-change, identity re-bound per generation, lineage signed and reversible.

Delegation

Trust across agent boundaries

The handoff is the confused deputy restated for agents. We work on attenuated short-lived capability, bounded delegation depth, and audit chains that survive a third hop.

Memory

Governed memory and retrieval

What a system is permitted to remember, who it is remembering for, and what retrieval owes an auditor. Identity-scoped stores compared against retrieval-only architectures.

Evaluation

Measuring computer-use agents

Task success hides the interesting failures. We score trajectories on the authority they consumed, not only on whether the task closed, building on public computer-use benchmarks.

Open problems

Unsolved, and worth your time.

If you have an answer, a counterexample, or a sharper version of the question, we would like to read it.

  1. P1What is the right admission-control primitive for a policy that modifies itself between evaluations?
  2. P2Can capability attenuation survive three delegation hops without a central gateway?
  3. P3What does tamper-evident lineage cost at production throughput, and where does the cost stop being worth paying?
  4. P4Is an authority-accounted trajectory score reproducible across labs, or does it encode the harness it was written in?
